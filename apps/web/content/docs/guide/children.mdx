---
title: Children
description: The birth and adoption lines, which tie a child to the marriage they were born or adopted into.
---

A child is a person like any other. What makes them someone's child is an
indented line under their `person` line, naming the **marriage** they came
from: `birth` for a biological child, `adoption` for an adopted one.

## Birth

```kul
person hiroshi  name:"Hiroshi Tanaka"  gender:male    born:1978-02-11
person yuki     name:"Yuki Tanaka"     gender:female  born:1980-06-30

marriage m_hiroshi_yuki hiroshi yuki  start:2005-09-20

person akiko  name:"Akiko Tanaka"  gender:female  born:2008-04-17
  birth m_hiroshi_yuki
person kenji  name:"Kenji Tanaka"  gender:male  born:2011-11-03
  birth m_hiroshi_yuki
```

`birth m_hiroshi_yuki` says Akiko was born to the couple in that marriage.
Her parents are its two spouses, and Kenji is her brother because he names
the same marriage. None of that is written anywhere else.

A `birth` line has no fields of its own. The date of birth is the child's
`born:` field. A person has at most one `birth` line.

Sub-statement lines must be indented, and belong to the `person` line above
them. Two spaces is the convention, and what formatting produces.

### Parents need a marriage

A `birth` line names a marriage, never a parent. If your record names a
child's parents but says nothing about a wedding, declare the marriage
anyway, without a date, and note why:

```kul
person john  name:"John"  gender:male
person mary  name:"Mary"  gender:female

# Not recorded as married; declared so the children have parents.
marriage m_john_mary john mary

person tom  name:"Tom"  gender:male  born:1962
  birth m_john_mary
```

A person with no `birth` line is the start of what you know of their line.
When you find their parents later, add the marriage and the `birth` line.
The person's own line doesn't change.

## Adoption

```kul
person carlos  name:"Carlos Mendoza"  gender:male    born:1973-02-18
person rosa    name:"Rosa Mendoza"    gender:female  born:1975-09-24

marriage m_carlos_rosa carlos rosa  start:2000-01-15

person bayani  name:"Bayani Mendoza"  gender:other  born:~1995
  adoption m_carlos_rosa  start:2007-06-01
```

An `adoption` line names the adopting couple's marriage. `start:` is the
date the adoption took effect; write it when you know it. If the adoption
later ended, add `end:` with that date. Otherwise it lasts.

Adopted and biological children are equal members of the family. On the
tree the only difference is the line to their parents: solid for birth,
dashed for adoption.

## Birth and adoption together

A person can have one `birth` line and any number of `adoption` lines. Each
one stays true; a later adoption doesn't erase the birth family.

```kul
person eduardo  name:"Eduardo Reyes"  gender:male    born:1962-03-11
person luz      name:"Luz Reyes"      gender:female  born:1965-12-02

marriage m_eduardo_luz eduardo luz  start:1988-07-09

person tomas  name:"Tomas Garcia"  gender:male    born:1958-06-25
person elena  name:"Elena Garcia"  gender:female  born:1960-10-14

marriage m_tomas_elena tomas elena  start:1985-03-01

person carlos  name:"Carlos Mendoza"  gender:male    born:1973-02-18
person rosa    name:"Rosa Mendoza"    gender:female  born:1975-09-24

marriage m_carlos_rosa carlos rosa  start:2000-01-15

person dalisay  name:"Dalisay Mendoza"  gender:female  born:1990-08-03
  birth m_eduardo_luz
  adoption m_tomas_elena  start:1994-04-20  end:1999-02-10
  adoption m_carlos_rosa  start:2002-11-30
```

Dalisay was born to Eduardo and Luz, adopted by the Garcias for five years,
and later adopted by the Mendozas. The tree places her card with the
Mendozas, her most recent family, and marks the other two with faded
copies of her card. [Reading the tree](/docs/reading-the-tree/) explains
those.

## When the dates disagree

Kul checks a child's dates against the parents': a child can't be born
before a birth parent, and an adoption can't start before an adoptive
parent was born. It doesn't check that a birth falls inside the marriage's
dates, because real families don't always oblige.
