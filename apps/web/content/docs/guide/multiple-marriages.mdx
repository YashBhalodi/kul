---
title: Multiple marriages
description: Remarriage after a divorce or a death, and households where one person has several spouses at once.
---

A person can be in any number of marriages. Each is its own `marriage`
line, and each child names the one marriage they were born into, so
half-siblings stay clearly apart.

## Remarriage

After a divorce, each former spouse can marry again:

```kul tree
person erik    name:"Erik Lindqvist"    gender:male    born:1970-01-22
person astrid  name:"Astrid Lindqvist"  gender:female  born:1972-10-08

marriage m_erik_astrid erik astrid  start:1995-06-17  end:2003-06-15  end_reason:divorce

person linnea  name:"Linnea Lindqvist"  gender:female  born:1998-03-29
  birth m_erik_astrid

person maja  name:"Maja Holm"  gender:female  born:1975-05-14

marriage m_erik_maja erik maja  start:2006-08-26

person oskar  name:"Oskar Lindqvist"  gender:male  born:2008-12-01
  birth m_erik_maja
```

Linnea and Oskar share a father, Erik, through two different marriages. The
tree draws Erik's card with Maja, his current wife. His first marriage stays
on the tree with faded copies of Erik and Astrid, so Linnea still hangs from
her parents' marriage; Astrid's own card stands apart until you record what
happened next in her life.

After a spouse's death, a remarriage looks the same. The first marriage
keeps no `end:`, because [a death is not a divorce](/docs/guide/marriages/#a-death-is-not-a-divorce):

```kul
person anil   name:"Anil Desai"   gender:male
person priya  name:"Priya Desai"  gender:female  died:1990
person rekha  name:"Rekha Desai"  gender:female

marriage m_anil_priya anil priya  start:1970
marriage m_anil_rekha anil rekha  start:1993
```

Neither marriage has an `end:`, so Kul treats Anil like the households in
the next section: he must be the first spouse in both, and the tree draws
both wives beside him.

## Several spouses at once

Kul records polygamous households directly. Two marriages that overlap in
time are written the same way as any others:

```kul
person khalid  name:"Khalid Al-Rashid"  gender:male    born:1950-03-29
person aisha   name:"Aisha Al-Rashid"   gender:female  born:1955-07-11
person layla   name:"Layla Al-Rashid"   gender:female  born:1958-02-23

marriage m_khalid_aisha khalid aisha  start:1975-05-04
marriage m_khalid_layla khalid layla  start:1980-09-16

person yusuf  name:"Yusuf Al-Rashid"  gender:male  born:1977-06-13
  birth m_khalid_aisha
person zahra  name:"Zahra Al-Rashid"  gender:female  born:1982-01-08
  birth m_khalid_layla
```

There is one rule. A person in two or more marriages that haven't ended
must be the **host**, the first spouse, in every one of them. The tree then
has a single place to draw that household from: Khalid on top, each wife
on the row below him, and each marriage's children under that marriage.

If a marriage lists the shared spouse second, Kul reports it and suggests
the fix, which is to swap the two ids:

```kul expect-error=KUL-R14
person khalid  name:"Khalid Al-Rashid"  gender:male
person aisha   name:"Aisha Al-Rashid"   gender:female
person layla   name:"Layla Al-Rashid"   gender:female

marriage m_khalid_aisha khalid aisha
marriage m_layla_khalid layla khalid
```

The rule only counts marriages without an `end:`. Once a marriage has
ended, it no longer competes for the host's place.
