---
title: Dates
description: Full dates, partial dates and circa dates, and why you never have to invent precision you don't have.
---

Dates appear in `born:` and `died:` on a person, `start:` and `end:` on a
marriage, and `start:` and `end:` on an adoption. They are all written the
same way, without quotes.

## How much you know

Write the date to the precision your source gives you:

| You know         | Write        |
| ---------------- | ------------ |
| The exact day    | `1975-09-03` |
| The month        | `1975-09`    |
| The year         | `1975`       |
| Roughly the year | `~1975`      |

The year always has four digits, and the month and day two each:
`1975-09-03`, never `1975-9-3`. The date has to exist, so `1975-02-30` is
an error.

`~` means "circa": the date is approximate, beyond what the written
precision says. It works with any of the forms, so `~1975-09` reads "around
September 1975".

```kul
person grandmother  name:"Lakshmi"  gender:female  born:~1925  died:1998-03
person mother       name:"Uma"      gender:female  born:1950-07-14
```

## Not knowing is fine

There is no way to write an unknown date, on purpose. If you don't know a
date, leave the field out. If you know roughly, use a coarser date or `~`.
You never have to make up a day to satisfy a form.

These say three different things, and Kul keeps them apart:

- No `born:` at all: the birth date isn't recorded.
- `born:1950`: born in 1950, day and month not recorded.
- `born:~1950`: born around 1950, give or take.

Comments are a good place for where a date came from, or for a source that
disagrees:

```kul
person grandfather  name:"Grandfather"  gender:male  born:~1925  # the family bible says 1923
```

## How Kul checks dates

Kul catches impossible timelines: a death before a birth, a marriage that
ends before it starts, a child born before a parent, a marriage after a
spouse's death, and a few more listed in the
[diagnostics reference](/docs/reference/diagnostics/).

Uncertain dates are given the benefit of the doubt:

- A partial date stands for its whole period. `1950` means any day in 1950,
  so a child born `1950` and a parent born `1950-06` is not an error: the
  child might have been born in December.
- A `~` date gets five years of slack either way. A parent born `~1955` and
  a child born `1952` is allowed, because the parent might have been born
  as early as 1950.

A rule only fires when the dates conflict however the uncertainty is read.

```kul
person mother  name:"Uma"    gender:female  born:~1955
person father  name:"Arjun"  gender:male

marriage m_arjun_uma father mother

person son  name:"Kiran"  gender:male  born:1952
  birth m_arjun_uma
```

Kul does not judge whether a date is plausible. A mother aged 12, or a
child born years after a divorce, is accepted: real records contain
surprises, and Kul records them rather than arguing.

What it does flag is a timeline that can't be true. Here Ravi dies before he
is born, which Kul reports as `KUL-R06`:

```kul expect-error=KUL-R06
person ravi  name:"Ravi"  gender:male  born:1950  died:1948
```
