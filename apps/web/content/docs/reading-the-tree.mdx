---
title: Reading the tree
description: What the cards, lines and faded cards on the canvas mean, and why each person sits where they do.
---

Kul draws every family the same way, from the text alone. There are no
settings and nothing to arrange by hand: the same files always give the
same tree. This page explains how to read it.

## The basic picture

The tree is a classic family tree. Each generation is a row, oldest at the
top. A married couple sit side by side, joined by a thick line, and their
children hang from the middle of that line on the row below.

```kul tree
person hiroshi  name:"Hiroshi Tanaka"  gender:male    born:1978-02-11
person yuki     name:"Yuki Tanaka"     gender:female  born:1980-06-30

marriage m_hiroshi_yuki hiroshi yuki  start:2005-09-20

person akiko  name:"Akiko Tanaka"  gender:female  born:2008-04-17
  birth m_hiroshi_yuki
person kenji  name:"Kenji Tanaka"  gender:male  born:2011-11-03
  birth m_hiroshi_yuki
```

Children attach to their parents' **marriage**, not to either parent,
because that is how a `birth` line is written.

## Cards

Each person has one card, showing their name. Every card has the same
shape and size. The colour of its outline shows gender, as the legend says.

Dates and other details are not on the card. In the app, click a card to
see everything recorded about that person in the details panel.

## Lines

- A **thick line** between two cards is a marriage. A marriage that ended
  in divorce is drawn fainter.
- A **solid line** down to a child is a birth.
- A **dashed line** down to a child is an adoption.

## Where each person sits

A person can belong to several families at once: the one they were born
into, one or more that adopted them, and each family they married into. Kul
draws their card in one place, chosen in this order:

1. With their first marriage that hasn't ended.
2. Otherwise, in the family that adopted them most recently.
3. Otherwise, in the family they were born into.
4. Otherwise, on its own.

So a married daughter sits beside her husband, an adopted son sits with
his adoptive parents, and someone with no recorded ties stands alone.

A couple sit in the family of the marriage's **host**, the first spouse in
the `marriage` line. The other spouse's birth family is drawn as a tree of
its own.

## Faded cards are past records

Every other place a person belongs gets a faded copy of their card, with a
dotted outline: a **past record**. It holds the lines of a family that
person was part of, without a second full card. It appears:

- with a marriage that ended, for a spouse who has since remarried;
- in an earlier adoptive family, for someone adopted again later;
- in a birth family, for someone who married into another family or was
  adopted.

Here Erik and Astrid divorced and each remarried. Their cards sit with
their new spouses, and a past record of each stays at their first marriage,
so their daughter Linnea still hangs from her parents' marriage:

```kul tree
person erik    name:"Erik Lindqvist"    gender:male    born:1970-01-22
person astrid  name:"Astrid Lindqvist"  gender:female  born:1972-10-08

marriage m_erik_astrid erik astrid  start:1995-06-17  end:2003-06-15  end_reason:divorce

person linnea  name:"Linnea Lindqvist"  gender:female  born:1998-03-29
  birth m_erik_astrid

person maja  name:"Maja Holm"  gender:female  born:1975-05-14

marriage m_erik_maja erik maja  start:2006-08-26

person oskar  name:"Oskar Lindqvist"  gender:male  born:2008-12-01
  birth m_erik_maja

person johan  name:"Johan Berg"  gender:male  born:1971-07-30

marriage m_astrid_johan astrid johan  start:2007-04-12
```

A past record only connects to the family it stands in. Everything else
about the person, such as their own parents and other marriages, connects
to their real card. Match the two by name. In the app, a past record has a
↺ badge in its corner; click it to go to the person's own card.

## Several spouses at once

A person with more than one current marriage is drawn once, on a row of
their own. Each spouse sits on the row below, joined by their own marriage
line, and each marriage's children gather under that marriage:

```kul tree
person khalid  name:"Khalid Al-Rashid"  gender:male    born:1950-03-29
person aisha   name:"Aisha Al-Rashid"   gender:female  born:1955-07-11
person layla   name:"Layla Al-Rashid"   gender:female  born:1958-02-23

marriage m_khalid_aisha khalid aisha  start:1975-05-04
marriage m_khalid_layla khalid layla  start:1980-09-16

person yusuf  name:"Yusuf Al-Rashid"  gender:male  born:1977-06-13
  birth m_khalid_aisha
person zahra  name:"Zahra Al-Rashid"  gender:female  born:1982-01-08
  birth m_khalid_layla
```

## Separate families

Families with no recorded link between them are drawn as separate trees,
left to right in the order they appear in your files, all sharing the same
rows for each generation. See
[disconnected lineages](/docs/guide/disconnected-lineages/).

## What the tree doesn't show

Time is not an axis: a card's position says nothing about when the person
lived, only how they are related. Brothers and sisters appear in the order
their `person` lines are written, not by age, so write them in birth order
if that is how you want to see them.

## The legend

The app's legend lists only what the current tree contains: the gender
colours, past records, and the kinds of line. Use the legend button in the
canvas controls to show or hide it. A tree you download as SVG carries the
same legend.
