Kul is a small language for writing down a family. You describe the people,
the marriages between them and which marriage each child came from. Kul
checks what you wrote and draws the family tree from it.

Here is a whole family in Kul:

```kul
person ramesh  name:"Ramesh Sharma"  gender:male    born:1925-03-10  died:2005-08-22
person sita    name:"Sita Sharma"    gender:female  born:~1928

marriage m_ramesh_sita ramesh sita  start:1948-06-10

person alice  name:"Alice Sharma"  gender:female  born:1950-04
  birth m_ramesh_sita
```

Three kinds of line carry everything:

- `person` introduces someone, with a short id (`ramesh`) that the rest of
  the text uses to refer to them.
- `marriage` joins two people.
- `birth` (or `adoption`), indented under a person, names the marriage that
  person was born or adopted into.

Nothing else is written down. Alice's parents follow from her `birth` line,
and so would her siblings, cousins and in-laws once you add them. Sita's
birth year is only known roughly, so it reads `~1928`. Alice's birth is
known to the month, so it stops at the month.

## Why plain text

Family history is messy. Dates are half remembered, people marry more than
once, children are adopted, and some branches never connect to the rest.
Genealogy apps ask you to fit all of that into their forms and keep it in
their database. Drawing tools give you a picture with no meaning behind it.

In Kul the text is the record:

- It reads like notes, so you can check it against what you were told.
- It is yours. It is a few small files you can copy, keep in version
  control, or send to a relative.
- Adding something you learn later is one more line. Nothing you wrote
  before has to change.
- Kul is lenient where records are uncertain and strict where meaning would
  be ambiguous. A missing date is fine. A child pointing at a marriage that
  doesn't exist is an error, explained in plain words.

## What the app does

The [Kul app](/app/) runs in your browser. You type Kul in a small editor
window, and the tree on the canvas behind it redraws as you type. Mistakes
are underlined with an explanation, and a few common ones come with a fix
you can apply in one step. Click a
person on the tree to jump to the line that declares them.

The app keeps your projects in your browser, on your computer. There is no
account and nothing is uploaded. [Where your files live](/docs/app/files-and-backups/)
explains what that means for backups.

It does not research your family for you, import from genealogy sites, or
model relationships other than marriage, birth and adoption. Friendships,
engagements and godparents are outside the language; you can still note
them in a comment.
